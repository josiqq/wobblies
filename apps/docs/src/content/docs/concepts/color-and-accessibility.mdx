## OKLCH

Colors are built in OKLCH (lightness, chroma, hue), a perceptual space where equal steps look equal. The hue comes from the name; lightness and chroma come from a *tone family* picked by another per-name trait. Results are converted to hex sRGB at the end.

## Tone families

The `tone` trait selects one of six families of body colors:

| Tone range | Family | Lightness | Chroma |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 0.18 | pastel | 0.88 | 0.07 |
| 0.18 to 0.32 | chalk | 0.92 | 0.03 |
| 0.32 to 0.60 | fresh | 0.76 | 0.13 |
| 0.60 to 0.78 | vivid | 0.65 | 0.16 |
| 0.78 to 0.92 | neon-ish | 0.85 | 0.15 |
| 0.92 to 1 | dark | 0.36 | 0.04 |

## Gamut mapping

Some hue, lightness and chroma combinations fall outside sRGB. Instead of clipping channels (which shifts the hue), the converter reduces chroma by binary search until the color fits, keeping hue and lightness.

## Guarantees

For palettes derived from a name:

- **Eye against body: at least 4.5:1** contrast (WCAG AA for normal text). Eyes start near-black on light bodies and near-white on dark ones, then lightness is adjusted until the ratio holds.
- **Body against background: at least 1.25:1**, so the figure never melts into its backdrop.
- **Body against near-black: at least 1.5:1**, so it stays visible on dark pages when the background is `none`.
- The `mad` mood tints the body toward red and re-checks the eye contrast at 4.5:1.

These hold for every name; they are not spot checks.

## What is not guaranteed

Custom `palette` values bypass the checks. Avatars are also identity images, not text, so the body/background ratio is intentionally lower than text contrast. For screen readers, give the avatar a [`title`](/wobblies/guides/react/#accessibility) or mark it `decorative` when a name is already visible nearby.
